---
title: "Research Chair in the Geopolitics of Seas and Oceans"
subtitle: "Canada Apogee Programme, Transforming Climate Action (TAC)"
axes:
  - id: axe-1
    title: "Geography of ocean volumes and verticality"
    items:
      - "Exploring the vertical interactions between the different strata of ocean space, from the seabed to the atmosphere, in order to develop a volumetric vision of the ocean that brings out its own spatial dynamics;"
      - "Integrating an Indigenous perspective and a fieldwork-based geographical approach, in order to decentre the gaze and document other conceptions of ocean territory;"
      - "Developing an interdisciplinary approach, in collaboration with TAC programme researchers and ISMER specialists, in order to bring the human sciences into oceanographic research."
  - id: axe-2
    title: "Governance of ocean spaces and adaptation to climate change"
    items:
      - "Analysing the international legal frameworks governing ocean spaces (United Nations Convention on the Law of the Sea, the Polar Code, agreements on the high seas and deep-sea drilling) along three dimensions, normative, actor-based and temporal, in order to understand how these regimes integrate (or not) the layered and shifting reality of ocean dynamics."
      - "Examining how the verticality of ocean spaces is taken into account in governance tools at the regional and local scales, through case studies."
  - id: axe-3
    title: "Geopolitics of maritime data"
    items:
      - "Analysing the conditions under which maritime data are produced (satellite, oceanographic, from in situ sensors, navigation or fishing), in order to document the power dynamics, actors and infrastructures involved in the making of knowledge about the ocean."
      - "Reflecting on issues of access, sovereignty and governance of maritime data among states, scientific agencies, research institutions and private actors, in a context of growing dependence on data for monitoring and managing climate change."
      - "Examining the social and normative implications of the production and circulation of maritime data, in particular for the coastal and Indigenous communities concerned."
      - "Analysing the power relations, international collaborations and data-governance issues that unfold in the conduct of an oceanographic expedition, by taking part in a scientific mission at sea."
  - id: axe-4
    title: "The scientist’s engagement in society"
    items:
      - "Continuing and deepening existing science-communication initiatives (notably publications for the general public and for school audiences), in order to make knowledge about ocean and climate issues more accessible."
      - "Translating research results for public decision-makers, notably through my participation in the *Science Meets Parliament* programme, in order to inform policy decisions on ocean governance and adaptation to climate change."
      - "Taking part in public dialogue (media, radio programmes, opinion pieces) to foster a wider circulation of scientific knowledge about the oceans and contribute to an informed public debate."
      - "Continuing reflection, together with the other TAC chairholders, on the sobriety and responsibility of scientific practice itself (travel, remote collaboration, methodological choices), consistent with the climate issues studied by the chair and the TAC programme more broadly."
